The 40 processes — the matrix you can actually use

Why this matters

Principles and domains are conceptual scaffolding; processes are where PMBOK 8 becomes operational. The 40 processes are concrete units of work — each with inputs, tools and techniques, and outputs — that a project actually does. PMBOK 7 removed the process catalog and many practitioners missed it: without processes, the framework is high-altitude and hard to apply. PMBOK 8's reintroduction of 40 processes (down from v6's 49) restores the practical bridge between framework and action. The number isn't the point — the existence of named, structured units of work is.

Key concepts

01

Processes are how the framework gets done

A process in PMBOK 8 is a named unit of work with three parts: inputs (what you need to start), tools and techniques (how you do it), and outputs (what you produce). "Initiate Project or Phase" is a process; so is "Manage Project Knowledge." Without this kind of structure, advice like "do good stakeholder engagement" stays abstract. With it, you have something to assign, review, and improve.

02

Each process has a domain and a focus area

Every process belongs to a performance domain (its topical home — Governance, Scope, Schedule, etc.) and a focus area (its temporal home — Initiating, Planning, Executing, M&C, Closing). "Initiate Project or Phase" lives in Governance × Initiating. "Plan Sourcing Strategy" lives in Governance × Planning. The matrix in Figure 4.2 shows the full distribution.

03

Governance is the only domain with processes in every focus area

Governance has roughly 9 processes spread across all five focus areas — the only domain that does. This is the structural reason governance "touches everything" (Lesson 3.1). Other domains concentrate their processes in Planning, Executing, and Monitoring & Controlling. Few processes live in Initiating or Closing — those are mostly governance and stakeholder activities.

04

Processes are non-prescriptive — adapt them

PMBOK 8 explicitly states that processes are not a checklist to be run linearly. They are units of work to be selected, tailored, and combined based on project context. A small adaptive project may meaningfully run 10 of the 40; a large regulated program may use all 40 in expanded form. Running all 40 on a small project is the v6 caricature PMBOK 8 was written to prevent.

05

Use the matrix to find the gap

The matrix isn't just reference material — it's a diagnostic. On any project, you can mark which cells you're actively working and which you're not. Cells you're not touching are either (a) intentionally tailored out, or (b) a gap that will hurt later. The discipline is to know which — and to be able to justify each "not running" decision out loud. "We didn't bother" is not a justification under PMBOK 8.

From the Standard

Why PMI reintroduced processes

PMI's rationale, drawn from their public-comment process: PMBOK 7 was widely seen as too abstract for practical day-to-day use. Practitioners reported using v7 as a high-level reference and falling back on v6 for actual work guidance. PMI concluded that the operational layer — concrete units of work with structured inputs, techniques, and outputs — needed to come back.

The reduction from 49 (v6) to 40 (v8) reflects consolidation, not deletion: overlapping processes were merged, and processes that turned out in practice to be sub-steps of other processes were absorbed. The 40 are also explicitly non-prescriptive — PMI's framing throughout is that processes are tools to select from, not rituals to perform.

Paraphrased from PMBOK 8 Appendix X5 (history of changes) and Section 1 commentary on processes.

In your world

Using the matrix for a project review

A PM doing a six-month checkpoint on a customer portal rebuild printed the 7×5 matrix and walked the steering committee through it. For each of the 35 cells, she said either "actively running" or "intentionally not running, because X" or "gap — we should be doing something here."

The exercise took 45 minutes. It surfaced four gaps the committee hadn't spotted in three months of status reports: no active stakeholder-engagement process in M&C (engagement was happening only at gates), no risk identification in Initiating for new sub-phases (carrying old risks forward without refresh), no formal scope change-acceptance in Executing, and no knowledge transfer cell in Closing.

The committee added owners to three of the four cells the next week. The fourth they consciously left empty — small enough scale that the overhead wasn't worth it. Either decision is fine under PMBOK 8; what mattered was that it became a decision rather than a default.

Processes Quiz

5 questions · tap an answer to check

What three components define a PMBOK 8 process?

A process has inputs (what you need to start), tools and techniques (how you do it), and outputs (what you produce). This is the same ITTO structure PMBOK 6 used — reintroduced in v8 after v7 removed it.

Why did PMI reintroduce the process catalog in PMBOK 8?

PMBOK 7's principle-only framing was too high-altitude for daily use. PMI brought back the operational layer — concrete units of work — while keeping v7's principles and domains.

How many processes does PMBOK 8 define, and how many did PMBOK 6 have?

40 in v8, 49 in v6. The reduction reflects consolidation — overlapping processes merged, sub-processes absorbed — not deletion. The structural concept of a process unit is unchanged.

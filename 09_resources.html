Performance Domain 6 of 7

Resources — getting and keeping the right people and things

Why this matters

Resources are the constraint that bites latest. By the time you know you're short on capacity, lead time has passed and your only remaining options are bad. PMBOK 8's Resources domain covers human, physical, financial, and informational resources — with explicit emphasis on overallocation prevention, because overallocation is the silent killer of project quality, schedule, and team retention. A project that finishes on time and budget by destroying its team has not succeeded under PMBOK 8's value-driven mindset.

Figure 3.6 — A resource histogram with overallocation flagged 3 sprints out. Level now or pay later.

Key concepts

01

Resources = people + things + access + skills

Headcount is the most visible resource but not the only one. PMBOK 8 frames resources broadly: people (with their specific skills), physical assets, financial resources, and increasingly informational resources (data access, tool licenses, security clearances). A project that's "fully staffed" but can't get the specific data access it needs is under-resourced, just less visibly.

02

Overallocation is the silent project killer

When more work is allocated to a person or team than their capacity, three things happen in sequence: quality degrades, schedule slips, and people leave. The first two get reported; the third gets explained as "normal attrition" but rarely is. The diagram above shows what mature resource planning looks like — overallocation flagged 3 sprints out, while there's still time to do something about it.

03

Leveling vs smoothing — same goal, different constraint

Resource leveling adjusts the schedule to fit available resources (the date moves). Resource smoothing keeps the schedule and finds additional capacity (the resource increases). Choose based on what's actually constrained. If the date is fixed, smooth. If the resource pool is fixed, level. If both are claimed to be fixed, escalate — somebody is lying to themselves.

04

People aren't fungible (skills matter)

Headcount math hides skills mismatch. Three frontend developers can't deliver a backend integration. "We have ten engineers" is meaningless without knowing what kind. Resource plans that count headcount but ignore skills routinely create allocations that look fine on a histogram and impossible in practice.

05

Plan for departures — they're certain

Over the course of a one-year project, somebody on the team will leave, take medical leave, or rotate. Plans that assume 100% retention of named individuals are fiction. PMBOK 8 explicitly mentions resource planning as including continuity provisions — documentation, cross-training, knowledge transfer — that pre-position the project to survive the inevitable transition.

From the Standard

The hybrid workforce in PMBOK 8

PMBOK 8 explicitly acknowledges that the modern resource picture is hybrid: full-time employees, contractors, partners, remote-first contributors, and increasingly AI-assisted workflows all coexist on a single project. The Guide treats this as a tailoring concern: resource planning that worked when the project was a single co-located team needs revision when half the team is remote, a third are contractors, and key analysis tasks are AI-augmented.

PMI also notes that human resource planning under PMBOK 8 should explicitly consider sustainability — both organizational sustainability (avoid burning out the team in pursuit of the milestone) and broader sustainability (consider environmental and social impact of physical resource decisions).

In your world

The histogram that saved a quarter

A program manager on a multi-project portfolio ran a resource histogram across all six in-flight projects every two weeks. In one cycle she noticed that three projects were going to peak demand for senior backend engineers in the same sprint — a peak that would put two of her people at 200% allocation.

She had three options: slip one project, smooth by hiring a contractor for the peak, or rescope one project to drop the backend-heavy work. She raised it in steering with all three options costed, before the peak hit.

The committee chose smoothing (contractor). It cost $40K and a week of onboarding. Not doing anything would have cost an estimated 6-week delay on the most critical project and, almost certainly, the departure of at least one of the two over-allocated engineers. The histogram is just bars; the discipline is checking it before the peak arrives.

5 Resources processes (Planning through M&C)

Technical depth

RACI matrix: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome), Consulted (provides input), Informed (gets updates). If a task has no A, nobody owns it. If it has two A's, nobody owns it. A RACI that assigns everyone as R produces diffusion of responsibility. Lead the Team is an explicit process in PMBOK 8 — not a nice-to-have footnote. It includes developing team capabilities, managing conflict, motivating, and building trust. Team leadership is a process you run, not a personality trait you have or lack.

Development Approaches & Life Cycles — predictive, adaptive, hybrid

Why this matters

Choosing the wrong development approach is the most expensive decision a PM can make at kickoff — and one of the hardest to reverse once the project is in motion. PMBOK 8 explicitly recognizes three broad approaches: predictive, adaptive, and hybrid. The choice is a tailoring decision, not a philosophical commitment. Most real projects are hybrid even when they don't call themselves that.

01

Predictive (waterfall)

Scope is well-defined upfront and unlikely to change significantly. Planning is detailed before execution begins. Work follows a linear or near-linear sequence through the Focus Areas. Best fit: regulatory builds, construction, compliance projects where the end state is known and mandated. The risk: if requirements are wrong or change, the cost of correction is high and grows with time.

02

Adaptive (agile)

Scope emerges through discovery. Work is delivered in short iterations (sprints, releases) with continuous stakeholder feedback. Planning is rolling-wave — detailed for the near term, sketched for the long term. Best fit: product development, innovation, anything where learning is faster than planning. Includes Scrum, Kanban, XP, and other frameworks. The risk: scope sprawl if "adaptive" becomes "no boundaries."

03

Hybrid

Combines predictive and adaptive elements — typically predictive governance and planning with adaptive execution, or predictive for well-understood components and adaptive for discovery-heavy ones. Most enterprise projects are hybrid in practice. The risk: incoherence if the two approaches aren't consciously integrated (running "agile" sprints inside a rigid waterfall governance model is theatre, not hybrid).

04

How to choose

Four factors drive the decision. Requirements stability: stable → predictive; evolving → adaptive. Stakeholder availability: available for continuous feedback → adaptive; available only at gates → predictive. Risk tolerance: high → adaptive allows faster learning; low → predictive provides more control. Organizational culture: command-and-control favors predictive; empowerment-oriented favors adaptive. Assess all four; the one in tension usually decides.

05

Life cycle ≠ approach

The life cycle is the series of phases a project passes through. The development approach is how work gets done within those phases. A project can have a predictive life cycle (sequential phases) with adaptive execution within the build phase. PMBOK 8 treats these as separate decisions — don't conflate them.

From the Standard

PMBOK 8's Standard (Section 4) and the Guide's tailoring section both emphasize that development approach selection is a tailoring decision, not a one-size-fits-all prescription. The Standard describes predictive, adaptive, and hybrid life cycles, and notes that the choice should be revisited at phase boundaries if conditions change.

Paraphrased from PMBOK 8 Standard § 4 and Guide § 3.
In your world

A PM running a platform migration defaulted to predictive because "migrations are waterfall." Six months in, requirements from the target platform's API kept changing with each vendor release. The team was spending more time on rebaselining than building.

She switched the integration layer to adaptive (two-week sprints, evolving backlog) while keeping the data migration itself predictive (fixed schema, known transforms). The hybrid approach cut rework by roughly 40% — because each component got the approach that matched its uncertainty profile, not the one the project defaulted to at kickoff.

The choice is a tailoring decision driven by requirements stability, stakeholder availability, risk tolerance, and organizational culture.

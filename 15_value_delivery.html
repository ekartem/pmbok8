Value Delivery System — where projects fit in the organization

Why this matters

A project doesn't exist in isolation. It sits inside a system of portfolios, programs, and operations that together deliver value to customers and stakeholders. PMBOK 8's Standard (Section 2) describes this system explicitly — and understanding it is what the "Adopt a Holistic View" principle actually demands in practice. Without this context, a PM optimizes a project that contradicts the portfolio strategy, irritates downstream operations, or arrives just as the market moves elsewhere.

01

Projects transform organizational states

A project moves the organization from a current state to a desired future state. The value of a project is the delta between those states — not the volume of deliverables produced. If the organization reaches the same future state without the project, the project has no value regardless of what it delivered.

02

Portfolios, programs, and projects nest

A portfolio is a collection of projects, programs, and operations managed together to achieve strategic objectives. A program is a group of related projects managed in a coordinated way. Your project lives inside one or both. Decisions at the portfolio or program level — budget reallocation, strategic pivot, resource rebalancing — constrain your project whether you see them coming or not.

03

Enterprise environmental factors constrain

Market conditions, government regulations, organizational culture, industry standards, political climate — these are enterprise environmental factors (EEFs). They're external to the project but shape every decision inside it. A PM who doesn't scan EEFs is optimizing inside a box they can't see.

04

Organizational process assets enable

Templates, lessons learned databases, historical schedule data, standard procedures, knowledge repositories — these are organizational process assets (OPAs). They're what the organization has learned from previous projects. Using them saves ramp-up time; contributing to them is how your project pays it forward. Mature organizations treat OPAs as infrastructure; immature ones let each project reinvent from scratch.

05

Value is business value, broadly defined

PMBOK 8 defines value broadly: financial return, market share, customer satisfaction, regulatory compliance, social impact, employee capability, brand strength. A project's value case should name which of these it moves and by how much. "We'll build X" is a plan, not a value case. "We'll build X to move metric Y from A to B" is a value case.

From the Standard

PMBOK 8's Standard (Section 2) presents the value delivery system as the overarching context for all project management. Projects, programs, and portfolios are components of this system; operations sustains the value that projects create. The Standard emphasizes that project management exists to create positive value that justifies the investment — and that understanding the organizational context is a prerequisite for doing so effectively.

Paraphrased from PMBOK 8 Standard § 2 (A System for Value Delivery).
In your world

A PM was running a customer portal rebuild when the quarterly portfolio review reallocated 15% of her budget to a higher-priority compliance project she'd never heard of. She was blindsided.

If she'd been scanning the portfolio context — understanding which strategic objectives the portfolio was optimizing for, and where her project ranked — she would have anticipated the reallocation and pre-positioned her sponsor to defend the budget or negotiate a phased cut. The project wasn't in trouble because of execution. It was in trouble because the PM treated it as a standalone effort in a system that doesn't have standalone efforts.

What is the 'value' of a project according to PMBOK 8?

Value is the organizational state change, not the volume of outputs. A project that produces deliverables without changing the organizational state has no value under PMBOK 8.

Enterprise environmental factors (EEFs) are:

EEFs are external conditions the project cannot control but must account for. They constrain every decision.

Organizational process assets (OPAs) include:

OPAs are what the organization has learned and formalized. Using them saves ramp-up; contributing to them pays it forward.

How do portfolios, programs, and projects relate?

Your project lives inside a portfolio/program structure. Decisions at those levels constrain your project.

A PM who treats their project as a standalone effort without scanning the portfolio context is violating which principle?

Holistic View means seeing the project as part of a larger system. Ignoring the portfolio context is the most common violation.
